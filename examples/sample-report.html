Tallyhand — Q3 Growth Plan

16 September 2026 · Six advisors · Blind peer review · CMO synthesis

The Verdict

REWORK

Success metrics were named but untargeted, and none connect to the paying-user objective — the measurement gate caps the verdict here regardless of everything else. Five of six advisors reached that independently without knowing a gate existed. The Performance Marketer's arithmetic is the strongest single objection in the room and all six peer reviewers ranked it first without coordination: the plan loses money per acquired customer at any creative quality. This is not a KILL — 180 word-of-mouth users in one niche and a fully-specified offer are real assets. The plan is recoverable; the document as submitted is not.

3 · Where the Council Clashes

Is the problem the message, or is the message irrelevant?

Creative Director

The fatal flaw is that nothing here would make anyone stop scrolling. Showing a screenshot of a form to people who make screenshots of forms for a living is a self-inflicted wound. Build the campaign around an idea.

Performance Marketer

The target is unreachable at any creative quality. $48 CPA against ~$70 contribution, payback at month 4.1, 40% gone by month 3. Better creative moves conversion within a range, and no point in that range fixes this.

All six reviewers ranked the Performance Marketer first and named the Creative Director's response the biggest blind spot — on exactly this ground. But note what they did not say: none called the diagnosis wrong. Every one said the diagnosis was correct and the prescription unaffordable. That distinction survives into change 3.

Is the podcast the best money in the plan, or the worst?

Creative Director + Brand Strategist

The strongest element — the only repeated exposure to a coherent audience where a distinctive brand association could form.

Performance Marketer + The Rival

A $222 CPA, nearly unmeasurable, 25% of budget committed to one month — and a competitor can buy the episodes either side for $2,500 and turn it into a warm-up act.

Not resolvable from the document: it gives no read on the show's size or the host's relationship to the audience. Reviewer 4 was the only reviewer who defunded it.

4 · Blind Spots Peer Review Caught

Retention is the lever, not acquisition

Four of six reviewers, independently. Every advisor treated 60% month-three retention as an input or a kill criterion; none treated it as the work. Fixing churn to 8% roughly doubles LTV and makes the same CPA survivable. Reviewer 4 sharpened it to the cohort nobody mentioned at all — the 40% who already left. This is the single most valuable thing round two produced, and it emerged four times from reviewers who could not see each other's work.

The $12 price went entirely unchallenged

Reviewers 1 and 2, independently. "F proves LTV is the binding constraint, then fixes CPA instead." A designer-specific tool at $29/month roughly triples LTV and repairs the payback math without acquiring a single additional user. Three advisors built the case for a price increase and none of them proposed one.

The objective itself may be the defect

Five of six reviewers. All six advisors accepted "1,000 paying users by 30 September" as fixed. It is a vanity number with no revenue attached. Would the founders accept 450 users at positive payback? A retention-first quarter reaching 500 users who stay is worth more than 1,000 who leak.

Convergence on the reallocation

Five of six reviewers defunded the same line — $8,000 out of Content and SEO, on the finding that 26 blog posts cannot rank inside 13 weeks. Reviewer 4 dissented and took it from the podcast. Every one of the six put the majority of the recovered money into retention, onboarding or referral to the existing base, and every one funded measurement and brand-term defence. Six independent allocations, one shape.

5 · The Brand / Performance Split

100% brand building100% activation

Roughly 75% activation / 25% equity — and the 25% is not really equity. $22,000 of paid social and search is direct response. The $8,000 of content is booked as an equity asset but builds the category's mental availability, not Tallyhand's, and returns zero Q3 users. It is neither. The podcast is the only genuine equity spend, and even that is latent.

The placement is accidental. A deliberate 75/25 is defensible for a 13-week revenue quarter — but the equity half is built from comparative positioning, and the activation half has no CPA, no conversion assumption and no kill threshold. Neither half was built to its own standard. The split is what fell out of dividing $40,000 across four channels that sounded reasonable.

7 · The Three Changes That Matter Most

  1. Replace the metrics section with a weekly CPA-and-payback dashboard, and set a $35 blended CPA kill threshold before any money moves.
    Raised by the Performance Marketer and the Operator

    Release $6,000 as a July test across two channels; release the remaining $34,000 only if measured CPA clears the threshold. First because it is the measurement gate's own requirement, and because every change below is unverifiable without it.

  2. Move $8,000 out of Content and SEO into retention, onboarding and the existing base.
    Raised by Reviewers 2, 3, 5 and 6 independently

    Spreadsheet import, first-invoice activation, dunning, win-back on the churned cohort, and a referral mechanism into the designer network that produced all 180 users. Second because doubling LTV via retention makes the current CPA survivable, where no channel optimisation does. Reviewer 4 dissents on the source — take it from the podcast — and that dissent is live.

  3. Narrow the audience to freelance designers, price to that niche, and make one claim not built from comparatives.
    Raised by the Brand Strategist, the Rival, the Customer and the Creative Director; the price test by Reviewers 1 and 2

    Test $29/month against $12. Validate with Reviewer 4's proposal: 15 paid interviews with current and churned users, which answers the positioning question, the Customer's "is anyone actually annoyed" question, and the LTV input at once.

Explicitly not carried forward: the Creative Director's public-data artifact. All six reviewers named it the biggest blind spot in the room. The diagnosis behind it stands and survives into change 3; the artifact does not — 180 accounts cannot produce credible aggregate data, 1.5 people cannot build it, and it carries a consent liability the response waved at rather than costed.

The One Thing to Do Monday

Open a spreadsheet and list every one of the 180 paying users and every customer who has churned since February, with signup date, last-invoice-sent date, and whether they are a designer. You will know by lunch what the real retention curve is, what fraction of the base is the niche, and who to call first.

The Performance Marketer 2/10

STRONGEST ELEMENT: The offer is priced clearly enough to compute against, and the $110 annual plan is a legitimate payback-compression lever.
FATAL FLAW: ~820 net new users from $40,000 is a blended CPA of $48.80 against an LTV of about $30 - every acquired customer loses money, and no line in the plan acknowledges it.
KILL CRITERIA: If 60% month-three retention is accurate and cannot be moved before spend starts.
THE FIX: Replace the metrics section with a weekly CPA-and-payback dashboard, set a $35 blended CPA kill threshold, and release only $6,000 in July.

Netting churn against the stated 60% retention puts the real requirement nearer 890 gross additions, so $40,000 / 890 = $44.90 CPA before Q3 churn. The Creative Director 2/10 
SCORE: 2 - A competent media buying schedule with no idea in it anywhere.
STRONGEST ELEMENT: The podcast is the only line item with a chance of producing something memorable - but nothing says what the ad would say, so even this is only latent.
FATAL FLAW: "Carousel ads showing the invoice builder" is the creative strategy for a product whose users are professional designers. You are showing a screenshot of a form to people who make screenshots of forms for a living.

The braver version: a public artifact - a running, anonymised tally of how long freelance invoices actually go unpaid. That is a thing designers screenshot, a thing the podcast host reads out unprompted, and a thing that earns links instead of buying them. What it risks: it is a bet on one concept instead of four hedged channels. The peer round unanimously rejected this prescription while accepting the diagnosis - see section 3.

The Operator 3/10 
SCORE: 3 - As a production schedule it is unbuildable: 1.5 people running four channels from a cold start with no asset count and no defined first week.
STRONGEST ELEMENT: The channel-to-month sequencing is staged rather than all-at-once, and the offer is fully specified, so a landing page could be built Monday.
FATAL FLAW: July alone needs roughly 50+ discrete assets against ~10 working days of design capacity, with one marketer acting as media buyer, copywriter, blog editor, analyst and podcast negotiator simultaneously.
KILL CRITERIA: If the designer's 50% is not ring-fenced from product UI work.
THE FIX: Cut July to Google Search only and spend week one on measurement and landing infrastructure.

Week one, concretely: Monday, stand up conversion tracking and define what a paying-user event is, because the metrics section contains no revenue number; Tuesday to Wednesday, one landing page plus five search ad variants; Thursday, keyword list and account build; Friday, live. The fourteen-day version is search ads against one page with trial-to-paid instrumented, producing a baseline CAC. Everything else waits for that number.
