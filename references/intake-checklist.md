# Intake Checklist

Run this before convening the council. It takes about a minute and it is the highest-value minute in the session.

A marketing plan, unlike an open question, has known required components. When one is missing, six advisors will each silently invent a different version of it and then evaluate their own invention. The output looks like six opinions but is actually six different plans being reviewed. The intake gate prevents that.

---

## The nine components

Assess each as **PRESENT**, **VAGUE**, or **MISSING**.

### 1. Objective
What business outcome is this supposed to produce?

- PRESENT: a specific outcome with a number and a deadline. "300 qualified consultation bookings by end of Q2."
- VAGUE: a direction without a magnitude. "Grow awareness." "Increase leads."
- MISSING: no stated objective, or the objective is an activity rather than an outcome ("launch a newsletter" is a task, not an objective).

### 2. Target audience
Who specifically is this for?

- PRESENT: a describable person with a situation, a trigger, and a current alternative. "Parents of Bangladeshi students in years 11-12 who have decided on overseas study but have not chosen a country, currently relying on word-of-mouth from relatives."
- VAGUE: a demographic bracket with no situation. "Students aged 18-24." "Small business owners."
- MISSING: "everyone who needs X," or no audience section at all.

**This is one of the two components most often missing. Flag it hard.**

### 3. Positioning / core message
What is the one thing this plan wants the audience to believe?

- PRESENT: a specific claim that a competitor could not truthfully make.
- VAGUE: a category-generic claim — "trusted," "expert," "leading," "your partner in X," "quality service."
- MISSING: no message stated, only channels and tactics.

### 4. Offer
What is the audience actually being asked to do or buy, at what price or commitment level?

- PRESENT: a named offer with price, terms, and what the buyer gets.
- VAGUE: "our services." "Book a call."
- MISSING: not stated.

### 5. Channels
Where does this run?

- PRESENT: named channels with a rough split of effort or spend.
- VAGUE: a list of platforms with no allocation.
- MISSING: not stated.

### 6. Budget
What money is committed?

- PRESENT: a total with at least a rough allocation across channels or phases.
- VAGUE: a total with no allocation, or "minimal budget."
- MISSING: not stated. Note that a genuinely zero-budget plan is PRESENT if stated as such — the Operator and Performance Marketer need to know.

### 7. Timeline
When does this run, and what are the phases?

- PRESENT: dates or a phased sequence with durations.
- VAGUE: "over the next few months."
- MISSING: not stated.

### 8. Success metrics
How will you know whether this worked?

- PRESENT: named metrics with target values and a measurement method that actually exists.
- VAGUE: metrics named without targets ("we'll track engagement"), or targets with no way to measure them.
- MISSING: not stated.

**This is the other component most often missing, and it triggers the measurement gate — see below.**

### 9. Owner and capacity
Who does the work?

- PRESENT: named people or roles with a realistic sense of their available hours.
- VAGUE: "the team," "we."
- MISSING: not stated. Often reveals a plan that silently assumes a hire.

---

## The intake table

Output this before anything else. Format:

```
| Component        | Status  | Note                                            |
|------------------|---------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Objective        | PRESENT | 300 bookings by end Q2                           |
| Target audience  | VAGUE   | "students and parents" — no situation or trigger |
| Positioning      | PRESENT | "the only consultancy that..."                   |
| Offer            | PRESENT | Free consultation, then paid application support |
| Channels         | PRESENT | Meta 60%, Google 30%, organic 10%                |
| Budget           | PRESENT | BDT 400,000 over 4 months                        |
| Timeline         | VAGUE   | "starting soon," no phases                       |
| Success metrics  | MISSING | none stated                                      |
| Owner / capacity  | MISSING | no owner named                                   |
```

---

## The branch

Count MISSING components only (VAGUE does not count toward the threshold, but is passed to advisors as a risk).

**4 or more MISSING — do not convene.**

Return the gap report and stop. Say plainly that the plan is not yet evaluable, list what is needed, and offer to run the council once the gaps are filled. Convening six advisors here wastes the session and produces confident-sounding output built on six different sets of invented assumptions.

**1 to 3 MISSING, or any VAGUE — convene with explicit unknowns.**

Include the intake table in the council brief and add this instruction to every advisor prompt:

> The following components are missing or vague from the plan: [list]. Do NOT invent values for these and evaluate your invention. Treat each as an open risk, and if a gap prevents you from judging your dimension, say so explicitly in your response.

**All PRESENT — convene normally.** Still include the intake table in the brief.

---

## The measurement gate

If **Success metrics** is MISSING or VAGUE, carry that forward to the chairman as a hard constraint:

> Success metrics were [MISSING / VAGUE]. Per the measurement gate, the verdict is capped at REWORK regardless of how the plan scores on other dimensions.

This is deliberately inflexible. A plan you cannot evaluate after the fact is a plan that will be argued about rather than learned from, and no amount of strategic elegance compensates. Enforcing this is one of the main reasons the council exists.

---

## Handling a plan given verbally

If the user describes the plan in chat rather than supplying a document, run the same checklist against what they said. Do not soften the standard because the input was conversational — a verbal plan missing metrics is still missing metrics. Reproduce your reading of the plan back in the council brief so the advisors and the user are evaluating the same thing.
